This ongoing research project develops η-regularisation as part of a broader programme toward a generalised theory of regularisation in quantum field theory. The central aim is to identify and classify new admissible classes of η regulators, understand the physical principles that constrain them, and determine when finite parts of divergent quantities are uniquely fixed by symmetry, analyticity, unitarity, or deeper ultraviolet structure.

A distinctive feature of the project is its attempt to build a bridge between fundamental physics and foundational mathematics, e.g., discrete mathematics and analytic number theory. Te η framework provides a way to reinterpret regularisation not merely as a technical prescription, but as a structured problem about the emergence of finite, physically meaningful quantities from formally divergent expressions.

Current directions include the classification of new η regulator classes in number theory, including in the context of modular forms, and with application in perturbative quantum field theory.

This project develops η regularisation as part of a broader research programme toward a generalised theory of regularisation in perturbative quantum field theory. Its central objective is to identify, derive, and classify admissible classes of η regulator functions, determine the physical and mathematical principles that constrain them, and clarify when finite parts of divergent quantities are uniquely fixed by symmetry, analyticity, unitarity, anomaly structure, or more general considerations deep in the high energy sector. Another important feature is foundations in analytic number theory, ranging from monomial series to modular forms.

Motivated in part by smoothed asymptotics and related methods, the η framework provides a setting in which regularisation may be studied not merely as a calculational device, but as a problem concerning the mathematical and physical status of finite remainders extracted from formally divergent expressions.

Current directions include the classification of new η regulator classes in number theory, perturbative QFT, and the field theory limit of string theory amplitudes; the study of gauge-invariant and anomaly-sensitive subclasses, the extension of the framework to the functional measure in the path-integral formalism, and the investigation of curved-spacetime and quantum-gravity-inspired constraints on admissible regulator structures.

Research questions: What makes a regulator physically admissible? When are finite values uniquely fixed? How can, if at all, number theoretic structures organise divergent quantities in QFT? Can top-down UV-finite structures constrain low-energy regularisation?
